# William T. Vollmann, *Poor People*: speaking positions and comparison

**Status:** preliminary source inventory; not a full-book reading. **Checked:** 2026-10-13.

## Primary material located

William T. Vollmann's *Poor People* was published by Ecco in 2007 (314-page first edition; the 2008 paperback has different pagination). The [book record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Poor_People.html?id=wfrb3hrWo6IC) includes the first-person framing and publisher description. The accessible [opening chapter excerpt](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/poor-people-reprint-vollmann-william-t/bk/9780060878849), titled “I Think I Am Rich” (Thailand, 2001), begins with Vollmann's encounter with Sunee in Klong Toey, Bangkok. This is **Vollmann reporting his experience**, not evidence that he interviewed a representative sample of Bangkok residents. Ledger `VOLLMANN-2007`.

## Why it belongs here

The book approaches poverty through conversations with people described as poor and through the author's own changing position as interviewer, purchaser, traveler, and observer. A person's own answer to whether they are rich or poor can conflict with an outsider's material ranking. This is a counterweight to treating every geographic or monetary ratio as a complete account of a life.

The research questions are concrete: Who can travel? Who asks the questions? Who has money to compensate another person's time? Who gets to leave, publish, or return? Which options are available to an interviewee? Do not infer personal motives or consent, or translate testimony into invented percentile scores.
